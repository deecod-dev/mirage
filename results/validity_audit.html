Framing

A high fingerprint AUROC is your hypothesis, not your problem

Worth being precise about this before anything else, because it changes what "inflated" means. PLAN.md claims that fault detectors "learn implementation fingerprints of synthetic fault injectors rather than underlying faults." Under that claim, a probe that identifies which injector produced a trace should score high. E4 scoring 0.909 is the hypothesis being confirmed. If it had come out at 0.55 you would be writing the null-result paper.

So the threat to the work is not that the number is too good. The threat is triviality: a reviewer looks at faults.py, sees that the grounding injector assigns trace["answer"] = donor["answer"] and that AGS is defined as answer · mean(context), and concludes you detected the field you just overwrote. That reviewer is right about two of your five classes. The finding survives only if the fingerprint persists once the direct write-then-read paths are cut — and right now it barely does.

The second threat is that the result has nothing to contrast against. The whole argument depends on the E5 transfer gap: fingerprint AUROC high, natural-fault AUROC low. You have zero natural faults, so there is currently no gap to report and the paper reduces to "we detected our own injectors."

Defect register

Eight findings, ordered by what they cost you

F1CriticalTwo classes are write-then-read tautologies

In faults.py, the grounding injector's entire body is out["answer"] = donor["answer"]. In metrics.py, AGS = answer · mean(selected_context). The injector swaps in an answer from a different question and the metric measures how well the answer matches the context. AGS alone separates grounding at 0.996.

Same shape for tools: the injector sets call["ok"] = False; TIHD_health is defined as failed_tools / len(tool_history). And TRTR_route reduces to tool_name not in {"calculator","document_lookup"} — a string lookup, which CLAUDE.md §3 already lists as a disqualifying pattern from the prior draft.

Evidence: grounding 0.999, tool_invocation 0.997 in the full probe; both collapse toward 0.63 when the coupled columns are dropped.
F2CriticalThe de-coupled probe fails G-4

G-4 requires the AUROC lower CI above 0.65 for at least one injector tier. Without the coupled features the point estimate is 0.6494 and the query-clustered 95% CI is [0.641, 0.659] — entirely below the floor. Per CLAUDE.md §4 rule 2 that is a binding gate failure, and kill condition K2 does not fire (it triggers at ≤ 0.60), so the branch table points at halt-and-fix rather than the null-result paper.

This is recoverable. It says the current metric suite minus its tautological members is weak, not that the hypothesis is wrong.

Evidence: 0.6494, cluster-bootstrap CI [0.6406, 0.6586], 1,000 resamples over 300 held-out queries.
F3CriticalRetrieval selects the entire candidate pool, so E5 has no natural faults

This is the one to fix first. HotpotQA distractor ships 10 candidate paragraphs per question. Your agent retrieves top-5 per subgoal and unions across 2–4 subgoals, so the mean size of selected_context is exactly 10.0. Nothing is ever filtered out. Retrieval is not retrieving, and every retrieval-sensitive metric (SCR, QCM, CDI_max) is measuring a set that always contains everything.

The direct consequence: natural_retrieval_failure is False for all 500 queries across E1, E3, and E4. CLAUDE.md §9 calls this label "a natural retrieval failure with a free label" and says it "defuses the strongest reviewer objection." It currently yields zero examples, against K3's 150-per-family floor.

Evidence: 0/300 clean E4 queries have no gold title in selected_context. Relaxing to "not all gold titles present" gives 11/300 (3.7%). Restricting to the union's top-3 gives 4/300. None of these reach 150.
F4MajorThe ten seeds are byte-identical

Every one of the 3,900 unique (query, condition) pairs in E4 has exactly the same 15 metric values across all ten seeds — maximum spread over any feature, over the whole run, is 0.0. The generator runs with do_sample=False, retrieval is deterministic, and the partition shuffle uses a hard-coded constant, so the seed changes only the config hash and therefore the output directory.

So E4's 39,000 traces are 3,900 traces written ten times, and the published CI of [0.9070, 0.9108] — width 0.0038 — is that narrow because the bootstrap resampled duplicates as independent draws. CLAUDE.md §3 lists "0% false positive rate from 3 repeats of a deterministic pipeline — zero variance by construction" as a disqualifying pattern from the prior draft. This is the same pattern at ten repeats.

Silver lining: re-running on the deduplicated set with a query-level cluster bootstrap gives [0.9126, 0.9214]. The headline is robust to fixing this — it just costs you the "ten seeds" claim, and about 90% of the GPU-hours spent on E4.
F5MajorThe T0/T1/T2 ladder does not exist for grounding

The grounding branch of inject() has no tier condition — all three tiers execute the identical out["answer"] = donor["answer"], with the same donor. I verified it downstream: for all 300 test queries, the T0, T1 and T2 grounding traces are identical vector-for-vector.

CLAUDE.md §9 states "the injector ladder T0 → T1 → T2 is the paper" and "never collapse the tiers." It is collapsed for one of four families, which is 9,000 of E4's traces measuring the same thing three times.

Evidence: grounding — T0≡T1 on 300/300, T0≡T2 on 300/300. Other families differ properly (query_retrieval 0/300 identical; source_degradation and tool_invocation identical only on 11 and 56 queries respectively, where the perturbation happened to be a no-op).
F6MajorTCS and SEO are the same metric

metrics.py computes "TCS": _mean_pairwise(trajectory) and then "SEO": _mean_pairwise(trajectory) — the same call on the same argument. Across all 39,000 traces, max|TCS − SEO| = 0 and r = 1.0000 on finite rows.

This is precisely the failure CLAUDE.md §3 records from the prior draft ("SCR and GSA reported as separate metrics… they correlated at r = 1.00"), reproduced with a new pair. E2, the metric-independence audit that would have caught it, has never been run. The suite is 14 metrics, not 15.

Also: both are undefined on 9.9% of traces and probe.py imputes NaN to −999.0, turning "inapplicable" into an extreme separable value. The rate is near-uniform across classes (9.3% everywhere except T1:query_retrieval at 16.0%), so it is a small leak rather than a large one — but it should be an explicit indicator column, not a sentinel.
F7ModerateT1 source degradation leaves a bit-exact signature

T1 replaces selected_context with copies of rejected_context but leaves rejected_context untouched. The two sets become identical, so QCM = mean(q·selected) − mean(q·rejected) is exactly 0.0 — in 100% of T1:source_degradation traces, and CDI_max collapses to exactly 0.0 as well, because deleting one passage always leaves an identical duplicate behind.

A floating-point identity at exactly zero is not something a natural fault produces. Any threshold near zero picks this class out perfectly, and it explains most of QCM's 0.797 on this family.

Evidence: QCM ≡ 0.0 on 3,000/3,000 T1:source_degradation traces.
F8ModerateTrivial baselines sit inside the probe instead of beside it

Three of the four M-D0 trivial baselines from PLAN.md — token_count, answer_length, and TRTR_route (tool name in registry) — are columns in the probe's feature matrix. That makes "do the novel semantic metrics beat trivial baselines" unanswerable from the current table, which is the comparison CLAUDE.md §4 rule 8 exists to force.

Splitting them out is good news, as it happens: baselines alone score 0.632 and the twelve non-baseline metrics score 0.869. The novel metrics do win. You just have to report the arms separately to say so.

Evidence: trivial baselines only 0.6315 · novel metrics only 0.8693 · all 15 together 0.9168.

Recovery

What actually proves robustness

Robustness here does not come from more seeds, wider bootstraps, or more traces — you already have ten times the data you need and it is all the same data. It comes from showing the fingerprint survives when the trivial explanations are removed, and from having a natural-fault contrast to measure it against. Four moves, in dependency order.

DayMoveFixesWhat it buys the paper
19–21 Make retrieval selective. Stop unioning selected across subgoals — score the final answer context only, or set top_k=2 and define natural failure as "not all gold titles present." If GPU budget allows, the stronger version is HotpotQA fullwiki, where retrieval failure is common rather than engineered. F3 Unblocks E5 entirely. Without this there is no transfer gap and no paper.
21–22 Decouple the injectors from the metrics. Grounding should perturb the generation, not overwrite the answer field — re-prompt the generator to assert one unsupported entity, then re-run verify. Tool faults should fail through real execution rather than by assigning ok = False. Give grounding genuine T1/T2 variants. F1, F5, F7 Turns "we detected the field we wrote" into a fingerprint claim a reviewer cannot dismiss in one paragraph.
22 Run E2. It is an afternoon: correlation matrix, VIF, drop exact duplicates, replace the −999 sentinel with an explicit is_defined indicator column per metric. F6 Removes the one defect that most resembles the prior draft's headline failure.
23–25 Re-run E4 as three pre-registered arms — trivial baselines, novel metrics, all features — with 300 queries, one seed, and query-level cluster bootstrap. Then run E5 against the natural faults from move one. F2, F4, F8 The gap between arm three on injected faults and arm three on natural faults is the result.

Days 26–29 leave room for E6 decomposition and drafting. E7's hardened injectors and E8's judge comparison are the first things to cut if time runs short — E5 is not, because it carries the claim.

On whether it is novel

Your friend is right that it is a good idea, and right that it is not untouched. Shortcut learning in synthetic-corruption benchmarks is well documented, and the closest neighbours are the work on hallucination-detection benchmarks whose synthetic negatives are separable by surface statistics, plus the general "clever Hans" literature on probing classifiers. What has not been done carefully, as far as I can tell from your PLAN.md framing, is the specific pairing: an agentic RAG trace, a tiered injector ladder from naive to hardened, and a quantified transfer gap to same-system natural faults. That is a real contribution — but E0, the prior-art hardening loop that would establish it, has not been run, and it is the cheapest experiment on the list.

The hardened T2 tool fault is the one to look at: it executes a valid expression with two operands transposed, so the call succeeds, the registry is clean, and it returns a real but wrong number. Both tool-health metrics are blind to it by construction — which is what makes it a fault rather than an assignment.

Two decisions the measurements forced

answer_top_k went to 2, not 3: a cross-encoder sweep over 300 real traces put the natural-failure rate at 11.7% for k=3 and 22.3% for k=2. Padding the candidate pool with random paragraphs from other questions did nothing at all — the reranker discards them — and hard negatives added only ~5 points before saturating, so pool padding was dropped to an E9 ablation.

At 22.3%, K3's 150-per-class bar needs about 670 queries, so the test split grows to 800. That is nearly free: the natural arm is a label on clean traces, so 800 clean runs cost one pipeline pass each while injection stays at 300 queries × 12 conditions. Whole plan: ≈11.5 GPU-hours against v1's ≈70.

Yes, and the pattern is the paper. Look along the grounding row: the naive injector produces an effect of −7.45, which is enormous — any detector will ace it. The faithful published-style version of the same fault gives −0.40. That is a nineteenfold collapse in detectability from changing nothing but the injector's implementation. Now read the top-right cell: the hardened tool fault — a valid calculator call with two operands transposed, returning a real but wrong number — moves every one of the fourteen metrics by essentially zero. It is completely invisible to the suite.

That is the thesis stated as a measurement rather than an argument: what these detectors mostly detect is how the fault was manufactured. And it is now demonstrated by construction, so the "you detected the field you overwrote" objection no longer applies — in v2 nothing writes a field a metric reads.

Two honest caveats. The real retrieval metrics do work: QCM at −2.30 and SCR at +1.67 on T1 source degradation are genuine large effects on a fault the pipeline actually experienced, and they survive into T2 at reduced size. But six of the fourteen metrics — IPS, MUT, SRS, GSA, ICRR, token_count — barely move anywhere, which is a finding E2 should report rather than something to hide. And this is 24 queries: directional only. The real numbers come from E3 and E4.

Three readings. First, on the tier the literature actually uses, these metrics are genuinely good — 0.83 to 0.98 AUROC on retrieval, source and tool faults, comfortably past your own G-3 bar of 0.70 macro-F1. They are not broken and the paper should not say they are. Second, the same metrics fall to 0.54–0.69 when the identical fault is injected in a hardened way, with tool detection collapsing from 0.975 to near chance. Third, grounding sits at 0.61 regardless of tier — this suite cannot detect hallucination, which is exactly why PLAN.md forbids claiming natural grounding without human annotation. That guard was right.

On comparability: there is no state of the art for the fingerprint number, because nobody publishes it — it is a diagnostic, not a benchmark. The detection numbers are comparable to published RAG-detector results, but that comparison is the trap the paper is about, since those published numbers are themselves measured on T0/T1-style injectors. Matching them is not the contribution. The contribution is that the number depends on the injector tier, and the tier is never reported. Making that concrete needs E8 (the symmetric LLM-judge baseline) and E0 (the prior-art loop) — both unrun, both cheap, and together they are what turns this table into a claim about the field rather than about one codebase.

Caveat that matters: 24 queries, so these carry ±0.02–0.07. The real numbers come from E3 and E4. One thing to settle before then — pooling all four families into one "is it faulty" label gives only 0.672, well under G-3's 0.70, while per-family detection passes easily. Decide which of those G-3 means, and write it down before you see the result.

One thing still needs your sign-off

CLAUDE.md §4 rule 10 demands five seeds minimum and ten for headline numbers. That rule assumed seeds produce variance; under greedy decoding they did not, which is how v1 paid ten times over for identical traces. v2 makes injection stochastic so seeds genuinely vary the faulted traces, and plans 3 for E3 and 5 for E4 with query-cluster bootstrap as the primary interval. That is a change to a pre-registered statistical choice, so §8 says it is your call, not mine.

The sentence you can defend today

On held-out HotpotQA queries, a linear probe recovers which synthetic injector produced a trace at macro AUROC 0.917 [0.913, 0.921]; removing the three metrics that read fields the injectors write directly leaves 0.649 [0.641, 0.659], and trivial length baselines alone reach 0.632.

That is an honest, interesting, publishable sentence — the ablation is arguably a better result than the headline, because it shows how much of a detector's apparent skill is injector-recognition. It is not yet the paper, because the paper needs the natural-fault half of the comparison. Fix retrieval first.
